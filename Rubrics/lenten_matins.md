# § INLINE RUBRICS: STANDARD LENTEN MATINS

Lenten Matins is served on weekdays (Monday through Friday) during Great Lent. It follows the skeleton of Daily Matins with several penitential interpolations.

---

## 1. The Enarxis (Opening) & Alleluia
**JSON nodes:** `opening_daily`, `alleluia_trinity_hymns`

The priest vests in the **epitrachelion only** (never the phelonion). The holy doors remain **closed**.
The physical opening choreography is identical to Daily Matins (the priest exits the north door, bows before the closed doors, exclaims *"Blessed is our God"*; he then performs the full censing of the holy table and church while the introductory prayers and Psalms 19 & 20 are read). Returning to the closed holy doors, he exclaims *"For thine is the kingdom"*, puts away the censer, and recites the opening litany. Finally, he exclaims: *"Glory to the Holy, Consubstantial..."* and the Six Psalms begin. The priest stands before the closed doors to read the Morning Prayers silently.
Instead of *"God is the Lord"*, the deacon intones the verses of **"Alleluia"** in the appointed tone of the week. 
The choir sings the **Trinity Hymns** (Triadica) of the tone. They are sung a total of three times, with specific Lenten insertions (e.g., *"Glory... Now..."*).

---

## 2. Three Kathismata with Prostrations
**JSON nodes:** `kathismata_lenten`, `sessional_1_octoechos`, `sessional_2_triodion`, `sessional_3_triodion`

Three Kathismata are chanted (rather than the usual two).
**Rubric:** At each intersection of the Kathisma (the *"Glory... Now... Alleluia"* section), the people make three small prostrations (metanias).

After the 1st Kathisma and Small Litany: The Sessional Hymns (Sidalnyi) of the **Octoechos** are read.
After the 2nd and 3rd Kathisma and Small Litanies: The Sessional Hymns of the **Triodion** are read.

---

## 3. Psalm 50
**JSON node:** `psalm_50_lenten`

Psalm 50 is read. Following verse 3 (*"For I know my iniquity..."*), the reader interjects the intercession verses:
☨ Glory: *"Through the prayers of the Apostles..."* (or the saint of the day).
Now: *"Through the prayers of the Theotokos..."*
Then the reader continues the Psalm.

---

## 4. The Canons
**JSON node:** `canon_lenten_triodic`

The Canons are chanted. The Lenten Canon uniquely highlights the **Triodion** (meaning "Three Odes").
While Odes 8 and 9 are always taken from the Triodion, a third Ode is taken corresponding to the day of the week (Monday = Ode 1, Tuesday = Ode 2, Wednesday = Ode 3, Thursday = Ode 4, Friday = Ode 5). 

---

## 5. The Magnificat & Exapostilarion
**JSON nodes:** `it_is_truly_meet_prostration`, `exapostilarion_lenten`

The priest exits the sanctuary, and censes the altar, the iconostasis, the choirs, and the people exactly as at Vespers. The choir sings *"More honorable than the Cherubim"* (The Magnificat).
Following the 9th Ode Katavasia, *"It is truly meet"* is sung. **One small prostration is made.**

The **Trinity Exapostilarion** of the tone is sung three times, with specific Lenten commemorations appended to it.

---

## 6. Praises and Doxology
**JSON nodes:** `praises_read`, `small_doxology_prostrations`

Psalms 148, 149, and 150 are read simply without stichera.
The Small Doxology (*"Glory to God in the highest..."*) is READ.
**Rubric:** 3 small prostrations are made at the reading of *"Glory to God in the highest"*.

Following the Litany of Supplication, the Aposticha of the **Triodion** are chanted.

---

## 7. The Conclusion
**JSON nodes:** `good_thing_lenten`, `trisagion_prostrations`, `standing_in_temple`, `lord_have_mercy_40`, `prayer_st_ephrem`

*"It is a good thing to give thanks unto the Lord"* is read **twice**.
The Trisagion and *"Our Father"* are read.
**Rubric:** 3 small prostrations are made during the Trisagion.

The dismissal troparion is replaced by: *"Standing in the temple of thy glory, we think we are in heaven; O Theotokos, the gate of heaven, open unto us the door of thy mercy."*

*"Lord, have mercy" (40 times).*
The **Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian** (*"O Lord and Master of my life..."*) is recited by the priest standing in the middle of the church before the holy doors. He makes **3 great prostrations**, followed by **12 small bows** (saying *"O God, cleanse me a sinner"*), concluding with the entire prayer again and **1 final great prostration**.

**There is no dismissal.** After the final prostration, the reader immediately says *"Come, let us worship..."* (3 small prostrations) and the First Hour begins.

# § INLINE RUBRICS: BRIDEGROOM MATINS

During the first three days of Holy Week (Great and Holy Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday), Lenten Matins becomes "Bridegroom Matins."

Instead of the Trinity Hymns at the "Alleluia," the choir sings *"Behold the Bridegroom comes at midnight, and blessed is the servant whom He shall find watching..."* three times in the Tone of the Day.

The full Canon from the Holy Week Triodion replaces the standard Lenten arrangement. The Menaion is entirely suppressed.

The Exapostilarion is the famous hymn: *"I see Thy bridal chamber adorned, O my Savior..."*

At the conclusion of the service (before the First Hour), the troparion *"Behold the Bridegroom"* is sung once more.
