# Sunday Evening Vespers — 4th Sunday of Great Lent
**Octoechos Tone 7 · Holy Martyr Agapius and the Six Martyrs with him**

> **Clarification on "Sunday Evening Vespers":**
> This is the **Daily (Lenten) Vespers served on Sunday evening**, which liturgically *opens Monday*. It is entirely distinct from the **Great Vespers** served the previous Saturday evening (which opened this Sunday). The two services have different forms, different stichera, and different propers.

> **On St. John Climacus:** Per Dolnytsky Part IV, line 448 — *"The service of St. John of the Ladder, which some later typika prescribed to sing on this Sunday, is usually not taken among us."* The primary source of this Typikon does not include his service in the normal parish observance. It is an optional insertion for monasteries only, replacing the regular Menaion saint. This digest follows the non-monastic rubric: **Octoechos + Menaion (Agapius) + no separate John Climacus service.**

## Service: Sunday Evening (Daily) Vespers
### Liturgical context of this service:
This vespers **closes Sunday and opens Monday** of the 5th week of Great Lent. It is a **Daily Vespers** (not Great Vespers). The Great Vespers for this Sunday was served on the previous Saturday evening.

### III. "LORD, I HAVE CRIED" *(Kyrie Ekekraxa)*

**Psalms sung/chanted:** Psalm 140 ("Lord, I have cried"), 141, 129, 116 — with the final verses of each psalm used as verse refrain for the stichera.

#### Stichera Distribution — **Total: 10 stichera**

> **Governing Rule (Primary Source):**
> Part IV, line 391–392: *"On 'Lord, I have cried' there will be 6 Sunday stichera of the Octoechos and 4 to the saint of the Menaion."*
> This applies to all Sundays of Lent following the 2nd Sunday rubric (which the 4th Sunday inherits).

| Count | Source | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | **Octoechos Tone 7**, Sunday Stichera | The 6 resurrectional stichera "upon Lord I have cried" for Tone 7 from the Octoechos Sunday cycle |
| **4** | **Menaion** | 4 stichera for Holy Martyr Agapius and the Six Martyrs |

> **Note on tone 7 stichera:** Since this is Sunday Evening (opening Monday), these are the Sunday stichera of Tone 7 at Great Vespers. These are the same stichera that were sung or referenced in the Saturday evening Great Vespers cycle for Tone 7; on the Sunday evening daily vespers the same Sunday Octoechos Tone 7 stichera continue to govern, now transitioning toward the following weekday.

#### Glory / Both Now

| Element | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Glory…** | Doxastikon of the Menaion *(for St. Agapius)*, if one is given in the Menaion for this saint | Menaion |
| **Both Now…** | **1st Theotokion of Tone 7** *(Dogmatikon: "It is meet to proclaim thee blessed, O Theotokos…")* | Octoechos Tone 7 |

> **Note on Dogmatikon:** The Dogmatikon of the current Tone is sung at the "Both Now" of "Lord I have cried" at the **Saturday Evening Great Vespers** (i.e., the Great Vespers that opened Sunday). For Sunday Evening Daily Vespers, the "Both Now" theotokion is from the Octoechos of the current Tone — it may be the Dogmatikon again or the general Tone 7 theotokion depending on local practice. Dolnytsky II.2 does not specify separately for Sunday evening; common practice uses the Tone 7 theotokion of the Sunday aposticha.

### IV. EVENING ENTRY — **OMITTED**

> **Primary Source:** Part IV, line 809: *"At Vespers: On Sunday evening for Monday there will be no Entrance, as happens on the Sundays of Lent (for here there is no Great Prokeimenon)."*

No Entry. No "O Gladsome Light" in solemn form (it may be read). No Great Prokeimenon.

---

> There is **no Great Prokeimenon** on Sunday evenings during Great Lent (Part IV, line 809).

### VIII. "NOW LETTEST THOU DEPART" *(Nunc Dimittis / Simeon's Song)*

Sung or read; the full Canticle of Simeon (Luke 2:29–32) followed by the Trisagion.

---

### IX. TRISAGION THROUGH "OUR FATHER"

Standard sequence:
- Holy God… (3x)
- All-Holy Trinity… / Lord have mercy (3x)
- Glory… Both Now… / Our Father…
- Priest: "For Thine is the kingdom…"

> **Prostrations:** On Sunday evenings during Great Lent the exact practice varies by local use. Strictly, prostrations (*great prostrations*) are **not made on Sunday** — the prohibition on Sunday prostrations is universal. However, since liturgically this vespers opens **Monday**, the transition point is debated. Per Dolnytsky Part IV, line 563: the Sunday evening vespers of Palm Sunday (analogous) uses "the same form as on previous Sundays of Great Lent" with prostrations at the conclusion. This suggests prostrations do resume at Sunday evening vespers for the subsequent weekday, but only **small metaniae** (not great prostrations) per the notes on prostrations (Part IV, lines 98–100).

### XII. LENTEN CONCLUSION — PRAYER OF ST. EPHREM

> **Primary Source:** This is prescribed for all daily Lenten vespers. On **Sunday**, as noted above, only small metaniae apply; the form follows the weekday pattern since this vespers liturgically opens Monday.

| # | Element |
|---|---|
| 1 | *"O Lord and Master of my life…"* — Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian |
| 2 | Great prostration (or small metanoia on Sunday) |
| 3 | *"O God, cleanse me a sinner"* (12x) with small bow at each |
| 4 | *"O Lord and Master…"* — repeated in full |

---

### XIII. DISMISSAL *(Lenten Form)*

The priest reads the dismissal prayer, then:

> *"Christ our true God, who rose from the dead, through the prayers of His most pure Mother, of our holy fathers, and of all the saints, have mercy on us and save us, for He is good and loves mankind."*

The specific Lenten dismissal formula, with the weekday commemorations (day's saint: Holy Martyr Agapius and the Six Martyrs with him; tone saint of the Octoechos).
