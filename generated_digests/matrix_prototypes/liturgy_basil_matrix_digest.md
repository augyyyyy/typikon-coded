# The Liturgy of St. Basil the Great (Matrix Prototype)
## Liturgical Digest

> **When served:** The Liturgy of St. Basil is celebrated exactly 10 times a year: The 5 Sundays of Great Lent, Holy Thursday, Holy Saturday, the Eves of Nativity and Theophany, and the Feast of St. Basil himself (Jan 1).
> **Governing logic:** The service is fundamentally similar to St. John Chrysostom, but the Anaphora is massively expanded. Structurally, it has three immense "Vesperal" hooks (where it is stitched to the end of Vespers instead of being served independently).

---

## II. THE ANOMALY MATRIX (VESPERAL HOOKS)
*When served on Holy Thursday, Holy Saturday, and the Eves of Nativity and Theophany.*

The Liturgy does not begin with *"Blessed is the Kingdom"* or Antiphons. Instead, it begins as a **Great Vespers** service that morphs into a Liturgy at the Little Entrance.

| # | Action | Condition / Fork |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Vespers Enarxis** | Priest exclaims *"Blessed is the Kingdom..."*, but immediately reads **Psalm 103**. |
| **2** | **Great Litany** | Read by Deacon. |
| **3** | **Lord I Have Cried**| Stichera of the specific Fasting day. |
| **4** | **The Entrance** | Made with the **Gospel**, not a censer. *(This serves as both the Vesperal Entrance and the Liturgical Little Entrance).* |
| **5** | **"O Joyful Light"** | Sung festively. |
| **6** | **[NODE C: Vesperal Readings]**| *See Vesperal Matrix below.* |
| **7** | **Trisagion** | *"Holy God"* (or *"As many as have been baptized"* if Nativity/Theophany/Holy Saturday). |
| **8** | **Liturgy Continuation**| The service immediately snaps to the **Epistle Reading** of the Liturgy, skipping Litanies, and proceeds exactly like a normal St. Basil Liturgy from Step 3 above. |

## III. THE BASIL VARIABLES (PART IV OVERRIDES)

### NODE A: The Anaphora of St. Basil
The Eucharistic Prayer is the defining feature of this liturgy.
*   **The Texts:** The theological depth and length of the Anaphoral prayers (recited silently by the priest) are roughly 4 times longer than Chrysostom's.
*   **The Choir:** To accommodate this immense length, the choir must sing the standard responses (*"A mercy of peace", "We praise thee, we bless thee"*) **extremely slowly and protractedly**. If the choir finishes before the Priest, they simply restate or drag out the final syllables until he elevates the Gifts.

### NODE B: The Megalynarion (Axion Estin Override)
St. Basil *never* uses *"It is truly meet"* (Axion Estin).
*   **Standard Rule:** The choir sings the magnificent hymn of St. John of Damascus: **"All of creation rejoices in thee, O Full of Grace..."**
*   **Holy Thursday Override:** *"Come O faithful, let us enjoy the Master's hospitality..."* (The 9th Ode Irmos).
*   **Holy Saturday Override:** *"Do not lament for me, O Mother..."* (The 9th Ode Irmos). 

### NODE C: The Massive Vesperal Readings
Because this service spans Vespers to Liturgy, it contains the largest Old Testament reading arrays of the year.
*   **Action:** Immediately following *"O Joyful Light"*, the clergy sit, and Readers chant sequence of Paremiya (Old Testament Prophecies).
*   **Holy Thursday:** 3 Paremiya.
*   **Nativity / Theophany Eves:** 8 to 13 Paremiya.
*   **Holy Saturday:** A staggering **15 Paremiya**. Between the 6th and 7th, the choir sings *"For He has been glorified"*, and between the 15th and the Epistle, the choir sings *"Praise the Lord and exalt Him above all forever"*.
