# Daily Vespers (Matrix Prototype)
## Liturgical Digest

> **When served:** Daily Vespers is celebrated on ordinary weekday evenings governing Saints of Ranks 4, 5, and 6, as well as the majority of Forefeast/Afterfeast days during the week. 
> **Governing logic:** The core variations rest entirely on the distribution of the Octoechos (the tone of the week) against the Menaion (the Saint of the day), mapping exactly to Cases 05, and 13-16 in Dolnytsky Part II.

---

## I. THE INVARIABLE SPINE
The Daily form omits all Entrances, Paremiya readings, and Vigil structures (Litiya/Artoklasia). The service flows linearly through these checkpoints:

| # | Action | Condition / Fork |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The Enarxis** | Priest exclaims *"Blessed is our God"* from before the closed doors. |
| **2** | **Psalm 103** | Read (Not sung). |
| **3** | **Great Litany** | Read by Deacon. |
| **4** | **Kathisma** | Read according to the weekly progression (e.g. 18th Kathisma). |
| **5** | **[NODE A: Lord, I Have Cried]**| *See distribution matrix below.* |
| **6** | **"O Joyful Light"** | Read (Not sung festively). No Entrance. |
| **7** | **Daily Prokeimenon** | Read or sung simply. |
| **8** | **Prayer of Vouchsafing** | *"Vouchsafe O Lord..."* |
| **9** | **Litanies** | Litany of Supplication (*"Let us complete..."*) |
| **10** | **[NODE B: Aposticha]** | *See distribution matrix below.* |
| **11** | **St. Simeon & Trisagion** | *"Now lettest thou thy servant..."*, "Our Father". |
| **12** | **[NODE C: Troparia]** | *See distribution matrix below.* |
| **13** | **Fervent Supplication** | The Litany of Fervent Supplication is shifted *here* to the end. |
| **14** | **The Conclusion** | The Daily Dismissal. |

---

## II. THE CASE MATRIX

### Case 05 & 05b: Weekday Simple & Six-Stichera Saints (Ranks 5 & 6)
*(The most common service of the entire year: an ordinary Tuesday, Thursday, etc.)*
*   **[Node A - Lord I Have Cried (6)]:** 3 Octoechos (Day of Week), 3 Saint (Menaion). Glory: Saint (if present). Both Now: Theotokion or Stavrotheotokion. *(If it is a 6-stichera saint, all 6 are from the Menaion).*
*   **[Node B - Aposticha]:** Aposticha of the Octoechos. Glory: Saint. Both Now: Theotokion.
*   **[Node C - Troparia]:** Troparion of the Saint. Glory/Both Now: Theotokion in the Tone of the Saint's Troparion.

### Case 10: Weekday Forefeast (Standalone)
*(The days leading up to a Great Feast)*
*   **[Node A - Lord I Have Cried (6)]:** 3 Forefeast, 3 Saint. Glory: Forefeast or Saint. Both Now: Forefeast.
*   **[Node B - Aposticha]:** Forefeast Aposticha. Glory: Saint (if present). Both Now: Forefeast.
*   **[Node C - Troparia]:** Saint Troparion. Glory/Both Now: Forefeast Troparion.

### Case 12: Weekday Afterfeast 
*(The days following a Great Feast)*
*   **[Node A - Lord I Have Cried (6)]:** 3 Festal, 3 Saint. Glory: Saint. Both Now: Festal.
*   **[Node B - Aposticha]:** Festal Aposticha. Glory: Saint. Both Now: Festal.
*   **[Node C - Troparia]:** Saint Troparion. Glory/Both Now: Festal Troparion.

### Case 13: Apodosis (Leavetaking) on a Weekday
*(The final day of a feast's celebration on a Tuesday, Wednesday, etc.)*
*   **[Node A - Lord I Have Cried (6)]:** 6 Festal Stichera. Glory/Both Now: Festal. *(The Menaion Saint of the day is entirely transferred to compline or another day).*
*   **[Node B - Aposticha]:** Festal Aposticha. Glory/Both Now: Festal.
*   **[Node C - Troparia]:** Festal Troparion only.

## III. FASTING INTERSECTIONS (PART IV CASES)

If Daily Vespers is celebrated during **Great Lent** or a **Strict Fast** (e.g. Beheading of St. John the Baptist):
1. **Node B (Aposticha) Override:** The Octoechos/Menaion Aposticha are entirely suppressed. The **Triodion Idiomelon and Martyricon** are used instead.
2. **Node C (Troparia) Override:** The Saint's Troparia are entirely suppressed. Instead, the choir sings the massive **Lenten Penitential Troparia** with full earth prostrations (*"Rejoice O Virgin Theotokos...", "O Baptizer of Christ..."*).
3. **Conclusion Override:** The dismissal is replaced by *"Lord have mercy"* (x40), the Prayer of the Heavenly King, and the **Prayer of St. Ephrem** with prostrations.
