3. Precedence / Combination Rules

a. Matins Dismissal Troparion
The Full Service Digest states: “At the Dismissal Troparia: We sing: Sunday Dismissal Troparion ‘Today salvation has come to the world’ in Tone I; Glory, Both now: Today Salvation.” This troparion belongs to the Feast of the Exaltation of the Cross (September 14) and has no place in an ordinary Sunday service. The proper dismissal troparion of Matins on a Sunday is the resurrectional troparion of the current tone, not a festal troparion.
- Citation: Dolnytsky_Typikon_Master.md – “Dismissals (Feast vs. Day Commemorations)” (Footnote 317); the Sunday dismissal after the Doxology is simply the resurrectional troparion of the tone, chanted once.
- Gap: The digests substitute a completely unrelated feast‑day troparion.
- Correction: Replace with the resurrectional troparion in Tone 4, sung once, without the unusual “Today salvation” incipit.

b. Praises – Incomplete Doxastikon / Theotokion
Both digests for the Praises read: “Glory... ; Other: Now and ever....” This provides no indication of which doxastikon or theotokion is to be sung. For a Sunday with a saint, the Glory is the doxastikon of the saint (if he has one) or the doxastikon of the Octoechos, and the Both now is invariably “Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Theotokos…” (for the resurrection service).
- Citation: Dolnytsky_Typikon_Master.md (Praises distribution, “Most blessed…” as the concluding Both now) and the glossary entry “Canon Structure …” where the exaposteilaria pattern confirms the Theotokion after Glory.
- Gap: The slot is left blank, making the rubric incomplete.
- Correction: Specify “Glory: Doxastikon of the saint (if provided); Both now: Most blessed art thou, O Theotokos, the salvation of the Christian race…” or the appropriate resurrectional Theotokion.

c. Exaposteilarion Section Misapplied
The Full Digest inserts “Exapostilarion: Holy Is the Lord in Tone 4” before the Eothinon Exaposteilarion. The hymn “Holy is the Lord our God” is not an exaposteilarion; it is a short troparion sung after the canon on Sundays, followed by the actual Exaposteilarion (Eothinon). The digests conflate the two, thus distorting the order of the canon’s conclusion.
- Citation: Dolnytsky_Typikon_Master.md – “Hymn ‘Holy is the Lord our God’ and its Verses” (Footnote 38), and the Canon Structure entry (Part 1, L176) which places “Holy is the Lord” right after the Small Litany of the 9th Ode, before the Exaposteilarion.
- Gap: The Exaposteilarion line is contaminated by a preceding “Holy Is the Lord” designation.
- Correction: Present “Holy is the Lord our God” as a separate item, then list “Exaposteilarion: Eothinon 1” (or the corrected number), followed by its Theotokion.
